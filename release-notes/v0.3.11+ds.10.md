## What changes

This release makes the listings producer, `darwin listings serve`, recover by itself when a node that left its census ignores the producer's socket while answering others. Nothing changes for uploads, downloads or any other command.

### Producer operators: why

- **A node can ignore one socket.** A node can keep ignoring every new connection from one socket (an address and port), as after a relay that socket reached it through closed, while it answers any other. The producer keeps one socket for as long as it runs, so such a node answered none of its probes and stayed out of the census until the producer was restarted; `darwin listings refresh-census` didn't help.

### Producer operators: the fresh-socket probe

- **What is probed.** Each cycle's census upkeep also probes the nodes that left 10 minutes ago or more and don't answer the producer's own probe, from a node started for that round alone: client mode, port 0, a new identity, the client's transport settings, no bootstrap peers. It is shut down after the round, and making, starting and stopping it are each bounded by 10 seconds.
- **How often.** At most one round every 10 minutes. When a round is due, those nodes are probed before the others, 64 at a time: the ones no round has probed first, then those a round probed longest ago, so the rounds reach every one of them in turn.
- **In the log.** Each round is logged at info level, for example `Census: 3 nodes out 10 minutes or more probed from a fresh socket; 1 answered`.

### Producer operators: starting again on a new socket

- **When.** If a node answers the fresh probe but not the producer's own, then once the cycle ends the producer logs a warning naming the nodes, stops as on SIGTERM (ending its watches and saving its peer cache) and exits with an error.
- **Run it under a service manager that restarts it** (systemd's `Restart=always`, for example): it starts again from its state, on a new socket. `darwin listings serve --help` says so.
- **How often at most.** A node's time out counts from the producer's start if it left before, so each start watches a node fail for 10 minutes itself first: a node that answers new sockets but never the producer's restarts it at most once every 10 minutes or so.

### Library

- New: `listings_census::probe_fresh`, `listings_census::FRESH_PROBE_AFTER`, `CensusChange::ignored_socket`, `CensusChange::fresh_round` (a `FreshRoundOutcome`), and `Producer::run` returns an error when the producer stops to start again on a new socket.
